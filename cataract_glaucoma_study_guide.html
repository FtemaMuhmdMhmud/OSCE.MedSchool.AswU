Cataract — الملخص الكامل

Congenital Cataract
Definition: Lens opacity present at birth or shortly after.

Causes:
  • Maternal malnutrition
  • Maternal infection (Rubella 1st trimester) → Total cataract
  • Deficient oxygenation (placenta praevia)
Symptoms (by mother): Defective vision, White pupil (leucocoria), Abnormal eye movements / nystagmus / squint

Signs (by doctor): White reflex by torch, cataract by ophthalmoscopy

Types: Anterior polar, Posterior polar, Lamellar (most common!), Coronary, Sutural, Blue dot, Total

Lamellar: Steering wheel appearance — Vitamin D deficiency → opaque fibers

Complications: Bilateral → nystagmus | Unilateral → amblyopia & squint

Management:
• Unilateral + affects vision → early surgery (avoid amblyopia)
• Bilateral dense → early surgery | Bilateral partial → surgery at 2 years
• Operative: Irrigation & Aspiration + posterior capsulotomy + anterior vitrectomy
• Postop rehabilitation: <2yr → contact lens/glasses | >2yr → IOL
• Occlusion therapy for amblyopia

⚠️ Total cataract (Rubella): Operation risky: cardiac anomaly (GA risk) + virus dormant 3 years → endophthalmitis
Senile Cataract
Definition: Bilateral, gradual, progressive lens opacity in elderly (no local/systemic disease)
Incidence: Age >50, males = females, bilateral

Etiology (4D + 1E):
  • Disturbed capsular permeability
  • Disturbed pH around lens
  • Disturbance of metabolites (↓ vit. C & glutathione)
  • Disturbance of endocrine hormones
  • Exposure to UV rays
Pathogenesis: Lactic acid accumulation → ↓pH → proteases activation → protein breakdown → lens opacity
Two changes: Hydration of cortex (water enters cortex → opaque) VS Sclerosis of nucleus

Types: Cortical | Nuclear | Corticonuclear

Senile Cortical — Stages:
  • Immature: partially opaque, iris shadow +, fundus visible — Symptoms: gradual ↓VA, uniocular diplopia, colored halos
  • Intumescent: hydration → swelling → shallow AC → phacomorphic glaucoma!
  • Mature: totally opaque, iris shadow absent, RR absent, VA=HM
  • Hypermature: shrinks (wrinkled capsule + cholesterol) | Morgagnian = nucleus sinks in milky fluid → IOP↑ (phacolytic G)
Senile Nuclear: >40 yrs, UV → photo-oxidation → brown pigment. Grades 1-4 (yellowish → brown → red → black = cataracta nigra). No hypermature stage!

Examination: Torchlight | Slit-lamp | Red reflex
Complicated & Traumatic Cataract
Complicated Cataract = opacity due to local or systemic disease. Posterior cortex affected first.
Local causes: Chronic iridocyclitis, Long-standing RD, Glaucoma, Retinitis pigmentosa, Degenerative myopia, Intraocular tumors, Drugs (corticosteroids, pilocarpine, adrenaline)
Systemic: Diabetes (snow flake OR presenile cataract), Hypoparathyroidism, Galactosemia, Irradiation, Naphthalene, Steroids, Chlorpromazine, Ankylostoma infestation

Traumatic Cataract:
• Perforation → direct damage
• Retained IOFB: Iron → siderotic cataract | Copper → sunflower cataract
• Blunt trauma → Vossius ring (iris pigment print on capsule)
Management: Suture rupture → remove IOFB → cataract extraction
Treatment of Cataract (Surgery)
Indications: ↓VA + lens-induced complications (subluxated lens, phacolytic uveitis/glaucoma, phacomorphic glaucoma)

Preoperative: Macular function test (two pinhole, projection of light, pupillary reaction) + IOP + US + ERG

Surgical options:
• ICCE: entire lens + capsule removed
• ECCE: 1. ECCE classic | 2. Phacoemulsification (40,000/sec ultrasonic) | 3. Phacolaser (erbium YAG)

Complications:
Operative: vitreous loss, posterior lens fragment loss, suprachoroidal hemorrhage
Early postop: iris prolapse, striate keratopathy, bacterial endophthalmitis
Late postop: capsular opacification (PCO → YAG laser capsulotomy), implant displacement, corneal decompensation, retinal detachment

After Cataract: Opacity in papillary area after operation or trauma. YAG laser capsulotomy if thick.

Glaucoma — الملخص الكامل

Aqueous Dynamics + Definition
Definition: Progressive optic neuropathy + characteristic visual field defect where IOP is an important risk factor.

Aqueous production: Ciliary body processes (non-pigmented epithelium)
Drainage: Trabecular meshwork (90%) → Schlemm's canal | Uveoscleral route | Iris root

Optic disc damage theories: Mechanical | Vascular | Combined
Congenital Glaucoma (Buphthalmos)
Pathogenesis: 1. Persistent mesodermal membrane (Barakan) | 2. Trabeculodysgenesis

Clinical picture:
Symptoms (by mother): Photophobia, Lacrimation, Blepharospasm → "Bull's eye"
Signs: Cloudy cornea, Haab's striae, ↑corneal diameter, Scleral thinning, Deep AC, Iridodonesis, Cupping, ↑IOP, Axial myopia

Examination under GA: Corneal diameter >12mm, IOP, Cupping, Angle

Management = SURGICAL (medical only adjunctive):
• Goniotomy (Barkan knife) — if angle visible
• Trabeculotomy (trabeculotome) — if angle not visible
• Trabeculectomy
POAG — Primary Open Angle Glaucoma
Most common type of glaucoma!
Mostly asymptomatic — accidentally discovered.

Risk factors: Age >50, Female, Black race, Myopia, Family history

Diagnosis (all 4 required):
  • ↑ IOP
  • Open, normal-appearing anterior chamber angle (gonioscopy)
  • Characteristic optic disc damage (cupping)
  • Visual function loss on perimetry
Treatment: Anti-glaucoma medications first → laser (trabeculoplasty) → trabeculectomy / shunt (Ahmed valve)
PACG — Acute Angle Closure Glaucoma
Risk factors: Hyperopia, FH, Age >60, Female, Asian/Inuit, Shallow AC, Shorter axial length, Thicker lens
Precipitating (mid-dilated pupil): Anxiety, Prone position, TV in dim light, Mydriatics

Pathogenesis: Iris bombe (most common!) | Iris crowding (rare)
Aqueous builds up → forward bowing of iris → closes angle → sharp ↑IOP

Stages:
1. Prodromal
2. Acute (Acute Congestive Glaucoma)
Ophthalmic emergency! IOP >50 mmHg, acute pain, N&V, halos, markedly ↓VA, cloudy cornea
3. Chronic
4. Absolute
5. Atrophic

Treatment of Acute Attack:
Hospitalize → supine position → Acetazolamide 500mg oral → Timolol 0.5% + Prednisolone 1% drops → Pilocarpine 2% → Analgesic + Antiemetic
If not responding → 50% glycerol oral / 20% Mannitol IV

After 48h (gonioscopy):
• PAS <50% → LPI (Laser Peripheral Iridotomy)
• PAS >50% → Trabeculectomy
• LPI in fellow eye prophylactically
Visual Field Defects in Glaucoma
ترتيب من الأخف للأشد:
  • Generalised depression of sensitivity
  • Isolated paracentral scotoma
  • Seidel's scotoma
  • Roenne's nasal step
  • Arcuate scotoma
  • Double arcuate (ring) scotoma
  • Tubular field (tunnel vision)
  • Temporal island of vision
  • Total field loss

الأمور المشتركة والروابط المهمة

Lens-induced Glaucoma (الرابط الأهم!)
العدسة نفسها ممكن تسبب الجلوكوما بأكتر من طريقة:
Phacomorphic Glaucoma Closed angle
المصدر: Intumescent cataract (cortical)
الآلية: العدسة تتنتفخ → تدفع الـ iris للأمام → تضيق زاوية الـ AC → angle closure
ملاحظة: يطلع في Immature stage
Phacolytic Glaucoma Open angle
المصدر: Hypermature cataract
الآلية: البروتين بيترشح من العدسة → الـ macrophages تاكله → تسد الـ trabecular meshwork → IOP↑
ملاحظة: أيضًا Phacotoxic uveitis
Glaucoma causes Cataract
الجلوكوما نفسها تسبب Complicated cataract

وكمان في Acute angle closure:
Glaukomflecken = spots على الـ anterior lens capsule due to epithelial necrosis
Colored Halos (في الاتنين!)
Cataract (immature): light scattering من خلال الـ opacities
Acute angle closure G: corneal edema بتكسر الضوء

الفرق: الجلوكوما → ألم شديد + IOP مرتفع جدًا
Congenital (الاتنين!)
كلهم ممكن يكونوا congenital:
• Congenital cataract → Lamellar most common
• Congenital glaucoma (Buphthalmos) → surgical condition

كلهم بيظهروا علامات على الأم لازم تلاحظها
Risk factors مشتركة
Advanced age → ↑risk لكليهما
Diabetes → cataract (diabetic/presenile) + neovascular G
Steroids → posterior subcapsular cataract + ↑IOP
Family history → مهم في كليهما

سؤال امتحانى محتمل: Patient with mature cataract develops ↑IOP

الـ D.D.:
1. Phacolytic glaucoma (open angle) — hypermature cataract + leaking protein
2. Phacomorphic glaucoma (closed angle) — intumescent cataract + angle closure
